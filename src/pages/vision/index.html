Computing that isn't confined to one machine.

The laptop you work on and the desktop with a capable GPU sit a metre apart and can't help each other. Every application that wants more compute builds its own networking, authentication and scheduling, or sends your data to someone else's servers. UCompute exists to change that, carefully.

Built, experimentalPlanned

An open computing layer

The goal is a layer that lets applications use authorized computing resources across supported devices, operating systems and networks, including applications that this project doesn't write or control.

It has three parts. A portable runtime runs supported work on each device and enforces its owner's policy. A protocol would let devices identify each other, agree what is allowed and exchange work, written down well enough that someone else can implement it. Today there is one implementation, and its peer protocol is experimental.{{claim:protocol}} A scheduler would decide where work should run, and say when the answer is “right here”. It is planned; today you choose the device yourself.{{claim:planned}}

Linux is the inspiration for the openness, portability and ecosystem reach. UCompute is not trying to replace an operating system. It is meant to run across them.

Every device plays a role

The specification defines four roles. A device can hold several, and none requires a GPU.

  • Consumer: submits work it is permitted to submit.
  • Provider: executes supported kinds of work for authorized consumers.
  • Coordinator: plans and supervises authorized work across providers.
  • Relay: carries encrypted traffic but can never authorize computation itself.

In October 2026 tests with development builds, a Mac and a Windows laptop acted as consumer and provider for each other. {{q:mesh}} There is no general coordinator or public relay yet.{{claim:mesh-pair-choose}}

Adoption is planned as experiments, not assumed. Each stage names what would prove it wrong. The private alpha is under way with invited testers; no stage has met its targets.{{claim:release-stages}}

One network, one credit balance

Planned

Our direction, set by the owner on 19 October 2026. Not started. Credits do not exist yet. Free does not mean unlimited.{{claim:direction}}

“Use your own compute for free. Share compute to earn credits. Let trusted people use your hardware for free. Spend credits to access compensated compute. Purchase credits when you need more.”

  • Your own computing stays free. Run, private Mesh and self-hosting are meant to cost nothing. Computing for yourself never costs or earns credits, however many devices or accounts you have.
  • Each device would choose how it shares, with sharing off by default. Private: only you and people you trust. Donate: free capacity for others, within your limits and schedule. Share for credits: paid requests, on your price floor, limits and schedule. Capacity you donate would never be sold.
  • Trusted people would use your hardware free. You would keep a list of the people and devices allowed to use your hardware at no charge, even on a device that also serves paid requests. Free access would come first, and a request would never move to paid capacity without the requester's say-so.
  • One balance for everything else. Credits earned by sharing, or bought when you need more, would pay for compensated compute from other owners. You would see the price before anything runs, and the platform fee would be shown openly. Free is not unlimited: free capacity is only what owners choose to share.
  • Cloud would stay optional. A managed service on the same open infrastructure, never required.

“Share your compute. Not your computer.” is the design goal for sharing with others: people would use your compute, never your files, desktop or accounts. The isolation that would make this safe with strangers is not built yet, and public participation stays disabled until it passes an independent security review.

None of this exists yetThere are no credits, purchases, payments, trusted-access lists, public Swarm or Cloud service today, and no stage that involves money is authorized. Transferring credits or cashing them out would be a later stage; it is not authorized and needs legal review. No cryptocurrency.{{claim:direction}}

Principles and non-goals

Local-first. Accounts optional. Secure by default: the API binds to this computer only, the engine's port is never exposed, and prompt text is kept out of logs (verified on an Apple M2 Mac, development build). No silent substitution of a model, precision or privacy mode. No fabricated benchmarks. No cryptocurrency.

Not on the plan for current delivery: a new operating system, custom inference kernels, running arbitrary downloaded programs, public anonymous access, mandatory accounts or tokens, always-on servers on phones, or claims that any model runs on any device.
